# Printing and assembling the penguin

Everything in this folder is generated by [`../generate.py`](../generate.py)
and committed, so you don't need to run anything to print it.

The enclosure is a penguin, in two colours: a **black shell** and a **white
belly** that the LEDs light up from behind. Assembled it's **91 × 55 × 117 mm**
and stands on your desk.

Five printed parts, six screws, six heat-set inserts. No supports anywhere.

---

## What's in here

| File | Qty | Filament | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `shell.stl` | 1 | **Black** | The body. Head, flippers, beak, belly window, eye sockets. |
| `backplate.stl` | 1 | **Black** | The rear panel. Everything electrical mounts on it. |
| `nest.stl` | 1 | **White** | Holds the six LEDs and reflects their light into the belly. |
| `eyes.stl` | 1 | **White** | Two plugs on a bridge. One part, both eyes. |
| `belly_*.stl` | 1 | **White / natural** | The diffuser. **Pick one** — this is the swappable bit. |

Four bellies ship ready to print:

| Belly | What it shows |
|---|---|
| `belly_blank` | Plain glow. The whole oval lights up evenly. |
| `belly_smiley` | A smiley face — the most legible of the four |
| `belly_chick` | A pixel penguin chick, on a penguin's belly |
| `belly_text` | Reads `AFK`; change it with `--text` |

### The plates

The `.3mf` files are pre-arranged plates. Open one in Bambu Studio and it
arrives laid out and correctly oriented — **don't rotate anything.**

| Plate | Filament | Parts | Rough time |
|---|---|---|---|
| `plate1_black.3mf` | Black | `shell` | ~5 h |
| `plate2_black.3mf` | Black | `backplate` | ~2 h |
| `plate3_white.3mf` | White | `nest`, `eyes`, `belly_blank`, `belly_smiley` | ~3 h |
| `plate4_white.3mf` | White | `belly_chick`, `belly_text` | ~2 h |

Plates are grouped by colour so a single-extruder machine never needs a
mid-print swap. Everything fits a **Bambu A1 mini** (180 × 180 × 180); the
largest part is the shell at 91 × 117 × 52 mm. If you have a 256 mm machine,
`python3 generate.py --plate 256` re-packs the whole set onto two plates.

You only need one belly to build the thing. Print the other three whenever.

### Print settings

| | |
|---|---|
| Layer height | 0.2 mm |
| Walls | 3 |
| Infill | 15% (`shell`, `backplate`), 20% (`nest`, `eyes`, `belly_*`) |
| Supports | **None.** Not on any part. |
| Brim | Not needed — every part has a large flat face on the plate |

Nothing in this design overhangs past 45°. That is checked, not assumed:
`generate.py` measures the downward-facing area of every part in its print
orientation and fails if any of it is unsupported.

> **The belly must be white or natural filament.** Its glyph is thinned to
> 0.9 mm while the panel around it is 2.6 mm, so light passes through the
> glyph and not the frame. A dark filament gives you a dim brown oval.

Printing the `nest` in white matters too — it's a reflector, and its whole
job is bouncing light. Print the `eyes` in natural/clear if you have it;
they pick up spill light from inside and glow faintly.

## Assembly

### 0. Get the electronics working first

On a breadboard, before anything is glued or soldered short:

```bash
cd firmware && pio run -e esp32s3 -t upload
cd ../host && pip install -e . && rookery test
```

On boot the firmware walks all six LEDs in order. Any LED that stays dark is
a wiring fault, and you want to find it now rather than after it's inside a
penguin. Full circuit in [`../WIRING.md`](../WIRING.md).

### 1. Fit the six heat-set inserts

Every insert in this build goes into a face you can rest an iron on top of,
straight down. Nothing is fitted at an angle or up inside a cavity.

- **Shell — 4 inserts.** Lay the shell face down, open back up. Four round
  bosses sit against the inside wall: two low, two at shoulder height. Their
  pockets face straight up at you.
- **Backplate — 2 inserts.** Lay the backplate flat, features up. The two
  tall pillars either side of centre are the nest posts. Their pockets are
  in the top of each pillar.

Technique: set the iron to about **200 °C** for PLA, sit the insert on the
hole, and let the iron's own weight carry it in. The pockets have a 45°
lead-in chamfer, so a straight start is easy. Pushing hard squeezes molten
plastic up around the flange and leaves the insert proud. Stop when the top
is flush and let it cool before touching it.

The pockets are cut **3.85 mm** across for a **4.0 mm** insert. That 0.15 mm
is deliberate — it's the material the insert melts into and grips. If yours
measure differently, `python3 generate.py --insert-od 4.2` and reprint.

### 2. Load the LEDs into the nest

Push the six LEDs up through the holes in the flat end of the cone, from
underneath, until they seat. Keep the colour order consistent so you can
trace the loom later.

> **Worth doing:** rub the dome of each LED flat on a bit of 400-grit
> sandpaper until it's frosted. A clear 5 mm LED throws a narrow ~20° beam
> and puts a bright spot in the middle of the belly; a sanded one scatters
> across a much wider angle and lights the whole oval. It costs two minutes
> and it is the single biggest improvement you can make to how this looks.

Bend all six cathodes together into a ring underneath and solder — that's
your ground bus. Solder each anode to its resistor, then a wire from each
resistor back toward the board. **Leave the wires long.** You will regret a
tight loom the first time you open it.

Trim the legs reasonably short and lay them outward: there's 3.3 mm between
the back of the LEDs and the tallest thing on the dev board.

### 3. Screw the nest to the backplate

The nest's two ears sit on the backplate's pillars. **2 × M3 × 1/4"**, from
the cone side.

The cone is the optics. Six single-colour LEDs 18 mm behind a 48 × 54 mm
window would otherwise be a bright spot with a dim rim; the cone flares out
to the full width of the window and bounces the spill into the edges.

### 4. Fit the dev board

It drops into the pocket on four posts and clicks under four retention tabs.
Press it down until it seats. It sits **upright, USB connector pointing
down**, so the cable runs down the inside and out the back at desk level.

Solder the LED loom to the board per the channel map in
[`../WIRING.md`](../WIRING.md).

### 5. Thread the cable

Feed the USB cable in through the slot in the bottom of the backplate
**before** plugging it into the board, then zip-tie it to the two posts
either side of the slot. That way a tug on the cable pulls on the enclosure
and not on the board's USB connector.

### 6. Drop the belly into the shell

From behind, through the open back. The belly's rim is a 45° cone and the
shell's window is countersunk to match, so it self-centres and seats flush
with the outside of the shell. It cannot fall out forward.

While you're in there, press the **eyes** in from behind too. They're
tapered — narrow at the front — so they seat from the inside and can't push
through. A dab of glue on the flange if you want to be sure.

### 7. Close it up

Lower the backplate assembly into the shell. The rim on the backplate locates
it, and the nest's cone lands on the back of the belly and presses it home —
that's deliberate, it's what takes the rattle out.

**4 × M3 × 1/4"** through the back into the shell's inserts. Snug, not tight;
you're clamping a printed part.

Rubber feet in the four recesses underneath if you have them.

## Swapping the belly

This is the only reason you'll ever need to open it, which is why all the
screws are on the back where nobody looks.

1. Four screws out, lift the backplate assembly off (mind the LED loom).
2. Push the old belly out from behind and drop the new one in.
3. Backplate back on, four screws.

Nothing gets unsoldered. The whole electrical assembly stays on the
backplate as one piece.

### Designing your own

Start from `belly_blank` and keep the lit graphic **small and central**. The
LEDs sit in a 15 mm circle about 18 mm behind the window, so a compact
centred icon lights evenly while a graphic that reaches the rim falls off at
the edges — the cone and the lens boss fight that, but they don't beat
physics.

For text, `python3 generate.py --text "BUSY"` uses a built-in 5 × 7 bitmap
font covering A–Z, 0–9 and a few symbols, auto-scaled to fit. Four
characters or fewer light most evenly.

If you'd rather model your own from scratch, the part that matters is the
rim: a 45° cone running from a 48 × 54 mm oval at the front face out to
59 × 65 mm over 5.6 mm of depth. Everything behind that is yours.

## How the belly is built, and why

Front face to back:

```
   0.0 - 2.6 mm    window plate, 45-degree rim -> wedges into the shell
   2.6 - 5.6 mm    the rim carries on at 45 degrees into the flange
   5.6 - 7.6 mm    flange, straight. The nest presses on this.
```

The middle is pocketed out from the back, which leaves a 2.6 mm window with
a faceted conical boss at its centre. **The boss is the point.** It puts
extra material exactly where the LED beam is strongest and tapers away to
nothing at the edges, so the thing you see is an even glow instead of a hot
spot. The glyph is then thinned from the back to a uniform 0.9 mm floor, so
it reads bright against the field.

The visible face prints against the build plate — best surface finish, and
the thin glow floor is laid down first with no bridging at all.

## Regenerating

`stl/` is committed, so you can print without running anything. To change
dimensions:

```bash
pip install trimesh manifold3d shapely numpy
cd hardware
python3 generate.py                    # rewrites stl/
python3 generate.py --insert-od 4.2    # if your inserts are fatter
python3 generate.py --led-d 3.0        # 3 mm LEDs instead of 5 mm
python3 generate.py --belly-clear 0.35 # if the belly is tight
python3 generate.py --text "BUSY"      # your own wording
python3 generate.py --plate 256        # A1 / P1 / X1: two plates instead of four
python3 generate.py --leds ring        # one WS2812B ring instead of six LEDs
```

`--leds ring` reshapes the nest to seat a 12-LED WS2812B ring instead of six
through-hole LEDs: three wires instead of thirteen, and no per-unit colour
trimming. The firmware already has that backend
(`pio run -e esp32s3-neopixel`). Measure your ring and pass `--ring-od` /
`--ring-id` — 12-LED rings are commonly 37 mm across but not universally. The
recess locates the ring; hot glue or foam tape holds it. It's the change to
make if you're building more than one of these — see
[`../PRODUCTION.md`](../PRODUCTION.md).

Every run checks itself and exits non-zero if anything is wrong:

- every part is manifold **after welding at the precision the file is
  written at** — a mesh that only holds together at full float precision
  will fail in someone else's slicer
- every part fits the plate
- no part has unsupported downward-facing area
- the shell's front skin bridges the cavity: the widest span is measured
- the screw arithmetic — thread engagement and clearance past the tip
- the assembled parts are boolean-intersected against each other, and
  against a solid standing in for the dev board, so a clash is a failure
  rather than a surprise at the bench

The two numbers you're most likely to need to change are `INSERT_OD`
(measure your inserts) and `BOARD_L` / `BOARD_W` (measure your board). Both
are at the top of `generate.py`.
