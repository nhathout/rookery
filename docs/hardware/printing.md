# Printing the penguin

Everything in [`hardware/stl/`](../../hardware/stl/) is generated by
[`generate.py`](../../hardware/generate.py) and committed, so you don't need to
run anything to print it.

It's a penguin built on a **10 mm voxel grid** — nothing is rounded, nothing
is filleted. A black shell, a white belly with six LEDs poking out of it in
a ring, and two cubes for feet. Assembled it's **80 × 60 × 120 mm** — it
stands next to a monitor without getting in the way.

**Six printed parts. Four screws. Four heat-set inserts. No supports
anywhere.**

---

## What's in here

| File | Filament | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `body.stl` | **Black** | The penguin. Head, flippers, feet, and the window the belly fills. Hollow, open at the back. |
| `belly.stl` | **White** | The panel the LEDs come through, and the plate you build the whole LED loom on — flat, on the bench. Carries the six bores, the resistor channels and the tie arches. |
| `chassis.stl` | **White** | The box behind it. Four walls and a shelf; it carries the dev board and nothing else. Snaps onto the belly. |
| `back.stl` | **Black** | Rear cover. Four screws, and the pillars that press the pair home. |
| `eyes.stl` | **White** | Two plugs on a bar, pupils cut right through. One part, both eyes. |
| `beak.stl` | **Black** (or orange) | A blunt wedge with a tang. Press fit. |

### The plates

The `.3mf` files are pre-arranged plates. Open one in Bambu Studio and it
arrives laid out and correctly oriented — **don't rotate anything.**

| Plate | Filament | Parts | Rough time |
|---|---|---|---|
| `plate1_black.3mf` | Black | `body`, `back`, `beak` | ~9 h |
| `plate2_white.3mf` | White | `belly`, `chassis`, `eyes` | ~4 h |

Two plates, one filament change, done. Everything fits a **Bambu A1 mini**
(180 × 180 × 180); the largest part is the body at 80 × 120 × 60 mm. On a
256 mm machine, `python3 generate.py --plate 256` re-packs the same set.

### Print settings

| | |
|---|---|
| Layer height | 0.2 mm |
| Walls | 3 |
| Infill | 15% |
| Supports | **None.** Not on any part. |
| Brim | Only the `chassis` wants one — it stands on a 1.8 mm rim. Everything else has a large flat face on the plate. |

Nothing overhangs past 45°. That is checked, not assumed: `generate.py`
measures the downward-facing area of every part in its print orientation and
fails if any of it is unsupported.

**The one ceiling in the build is the shelf inside the chassis**, and it is
worth knowing how it is held up, because a plate spanning an open box is
exactly the thing that quietly needs support:

- The loom routes through it are **windows, not slots out to the wall**, so
  the shelf stays welded to both side walls down their whole length.
- A **4 mm rib** runs down the middle of the cavity from the build plate to
  the shelf and carries its middle.

Between them, nothing on that shelf is ever more than **8.6 mm from
something holding it up** — a 17 mm bridge at worst. `generate.py` measures
that on every run.

The LED well is *not* a ceiling: it is cut into the back of the belly's
front wall, and the back faces up on the plate, so it prints as an open
pocket. And the chassis is a tube open at both ends, so the cavity under
the shelf is open to the build plate — there is nowhere in either part for
support material to get trapped even if you did switch supports on.

> **The `belly` is the part that has to be white.** It is the panel you
> see through the body's window. The `chassis` behind it is never visible
> from any angle once the thing is shut, so print it in whatever you have
> most of.

**Filament:** about 105 g black and 57 g white for one complete penguin.

## Choosing the face

The belly is plain by default: 4 × 6 pixels of the same voxel grid as the
body, with the six LEDs coming through the middle of it. That's what
`plate2_white.3mf` contains.

You can engrave a glyph into it as well:

```bash
python3 generate.py --face smiley      # a smiley face
python3 generate.py --face chick       # a pixel penguin chick
python3 generate.py --face text --text "BUSY"
```

Cut 1 mm into the front face, and automatically kept clear of the LED well —
there is 5.6 mm of plate and six LEDs in there. It is **decoration now, not
light**: when the LEDs shone through the belly a glyph could be thinned to
glow, and it can't be any more.

**This is a print-time choice, not a swap** — but it's a cheap one now.
Only the `belly` changes, so it's a 27 g print, and the LEDs and the loom
move across to the new one without touching the board or the box.

### If you'd rather have a glowing panel

`python3 generate.py --leds ring` builds the other version: one WS2812B ring
on the shelf, and the belly thinned to a 34 mm window 1.2 mm thick in front
of it. Three wires instead of thirteen, no resistors, no combs — and a
diffused glowing circle instead of six domes. The firmware already has the
backend (`env:esp32s3-neopixel`). With `--face` as well, the glyph itself
becomes the window.
